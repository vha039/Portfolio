Deliverable

Modernising the hospital's budget tool

The hospital's budget work rests on an Excel and VBA tool that is about 20 years old. Staffing data had to be copied in from another workbook. In the 2025 modernisation, in a process with about 50 participants, I integrated employee, employment and staffing data into the tool itself. I was the only person who coded the changes, both then and in the next round. The whole hospital has used the tool in the budget processes for 2026 and 2027.

My role

#
Code and integration
I was the only person who coded the changes, both in the 2025 modernisation and in the round for the 2027 budget process. I reconstructed the workbook's dependencies from VBA code, formulas and named ranges, integrated staffing data and compared the reworked functions with the originals.
Collaboration, training and support
Controllers and others involved took part in clarifying needs and in the budget process. I produced training and presentation material for the users. In the budget processes for 2026 and 2027 I was responsible for technical support and troubleshooting, and I helped users resolve the errors and problems that came up in use.

Used across the whole hospital in the budget processes for 2026 and 2027.

More on status
Delivery
The 2025 modernisation was delivered under strict compatibility requirements. The next round, for the 2027 budget process, is also complete. There I had substantially more freedom in the design.
Effect
Time savings and error reduction have not been quantified.

What an established workflow demands

#

The tool was business-critical and had grown over about 20 years. Its logic was spread across formulas, named ranges, macros and copies of workbooks. I had to understand it before I could change it, and the users' familiar working surface was part of the requirements.

In 2025 the implementation lead limited the work to function-by-function refactoring. I had recommended a broader restructuring and carried out the changes within the agreed scope. About 80 percent of the changes were refactoring that preserved existing behaviour.

The staffing data integration had to keep employees, employments, organisation and budget together, while period allocation, checks and export kept working in the established process.

Compatibility, staffing data and reconciliation

#

Preserving the workflow during the rebuild

#

The budget process had to carry on while the tool was being changed.

I mapped the dependencies between formulas, named ranges, macros and worksheets, and refactored the functions within the agreed scope. Along the way I compared old and reworked functions, using acceptance criteria and a register of deviations and risks. The comparison uncovered deviations in the calculation logic.

Compatibility was a condition of delivery. Each change had to be assessed against the behaviour that formulas, macros and users depended on. The broader rebuild I had recommended would have consolidated more of the logic, but it would have changed more parts of the working process at the same time.

Function-by-function refactoring let some inherited structures live on. In the round for the 2027 budget process I had substantially more room in the design.

Integrating staffing into the budget basis

#

Staffing information previously had to be copied in from another workbook, with manual steps and dependencies between files.

I integrated employee, employment and staffing data into the budget tool and linked it to organisation, finance, FTEs, period allocation, checks and export.

When the staffing basis lives in the tool, the link between source and calculation becomes part of the tool, not a copying routine users have to remember.

More combined information places greater demands on organisational links, periods and data quality.

Making deviations easy to find

#

Rounding and period allocation can create a difference between subtotals and the total, even when each calculation looks reasonable.

I improved the rounding and validation logic, the logging and the period allocation keys, and checked the changes against established behaviour.

Reconciliation shows where a difference arises. It matters most when the same value is carried on into several parts of the workbook. Checking each function separately would not have shown whether periods, totals and export still held together.

Sources and limits

#

Workbooks, code and internal documents are not published.

  • I did not create the original tool and I do not own the budget process.
  • The budget process for 2027 is the budget work in autumn 2026, not accounting results for 2027.
  • The worked example shows preservation of the total with fictitious figures. It is not a test of the original workbook.

Technical basis

#
Comparing against a known reference

I reconstructed the workbook's architecture from VBA code, metadata, formulas, named ranges and earlier reports. The 2025 workbook had about 15 worksheets and 78 global names.

A meaningful comparison uses the same basis and examines the outputs the change touches. A changed order, different key types or different rounding can move a difference to a later step.

I introduced mandatory variable declaration, collected constants in one place and wrote clearer comments in Norwegian. The checking used code and function comparison, workbook comparison and limited acceptance criteria.

An explicit remainder

The example distributes a combined, additive quantity of FTEs across periods, calculated in tenths. The period figures add up to the stated total. They do not show average staffing per month.

The first periods get an equal share rounded down, and the last period gets the remainder. This preserves the total without a negative remainder. The cost is that the whole remainder lands in the last period, which can skew the result for small totals.

Newly written example. It shows the pattern, not the original code.

// Total and number of periods are validated before the calculation.
const totalInTenths = Math.round(fte * 10);
const baseShare = Math.floor(totalInTenths / periods);
const lastPeriod = totalInTenths - baseShare * (periods - 1);

// The demonstration uses the shared calculation function.
// arsverk = FTEs, perioder = periods (the site's own parameter names).
const result = R8Calc.budget({ arsverk: fte, perioder: periods });
